Algotrader Strategy Scorecard

All 35 implemented strategies, each tested out of sample over 3.3 years on two universes of US stocks and ETFs: core (27 well-known large caps and index ETFs) and broad (107 tickers: core plus sector ETFs and a random sample, including names that later stopped trading). Section 0 lists every ticker; section 6 repeats the test over 14.8 years (2012 – 2026) on S&P 500 members. The test period is 3 July 2023 to 15 October 2026: 826 trading days, 3.3 years. Returns are given per day, week, month and year as compound averages over that period (a trading week is 5 days, a month 21, a year 252). Every number on this page comes from the committed results (results/, commit 328abef) or from a tuning check run on 2026-10-17.

Verdict

Many strategies make money; none clearly beats holding the market

    0 · What was tested

    Universes, dates and the benchmark

    Every strategy runs on the same tickers over the same dates, and is compared with simply buying all of those tickers and holding them.

    Timeline

    Data: 1-minute bars from 7 March 2022 to 15 October 2026 (HF Data Library, built from IEX trades). The first 16 months are only used for tuning and indicator warm-up. Results come from six test periods of six months each, joined end to end. At each test start, a strategy's settings are chosen using only the data before that date, so every result on this page is out of sample.

    Core universe: 27 tickers

    Today's well-known large caps, chosen in 2026, so it has survivorship bias (these are companies we already know survived and did well). That is why strategies are compared with buy and hold of the same tickers, not with zero.

    Broad universe: 107 tickers

    Core, plus 11 sector, bond and gold ETFs, plus 69 tickers drawn at random (fixed seed) from the provider's ~1,250 symbols. The random draw was filtered using March 2022 data only: trading by April 2022, price at least $5, at least 100 traded minutes a day. It includes losers and 7 names that stopped trading (acquired or renamed), which leave the portfolio at their last price. It is closer to what a strategy would face without hindsight.

    Buy and hold: the benchmark

    Each ticker's return from the first open (3 Jul 2023) to the last close (15 Oct 2026), or to its last trade if it stopped trading. Prices are split-adjusted; dividends are not included (for strategies or buy and hold).

    The luck bar: how good a result must be before it means anything

    If you try many strategies that have no real edge, some will still look good over a short test just by chance, and the more you try the better the best one looks. The luck bar is the Sharpe ratio that the best of N zero-edge strategies is expected to reach over this test length. A result below it is what luck alone would produce.

    Formula (Bailey & López de Prado, 2014, "The deflated Sharpe ratio"):

    luck bar = SE × [ (1 − γ) · z(1 − 1/N) + γ · z(1 − 1/(N·e)) ]

      5 · Tuning

      Does choosing parameters help?

      The walk-forward picks parameters at each fold from earlier data only. To see what that choice is worth, every grid point of the 19 rule-based daily strategies was run out of sample through the same six folds, held fixed. If tuning worked, the walk-forward dot would sit near the right end of the grid's range, and the in-sample ranking of grid points would predict their out-of-sample ranking.

      Tuning statistics

      "WF beats" is the share of fixed grid points the walk-forward choice beat out of sample. "Rank corr" is the mean Spearman correlation, across the six folds, between a grid point's in-sample score (used to choose) and its out-of-sample Sharpe: 1 means the in-sample ranking predicts perfectly, 0 means it carries no information.

          Method

          How these numbers were made

          Data: 1-minute IEX bars from the HF Data Library, March 2022 to October 2026. Six walk-forward folds start every six months from July 2023; parameters are chosen at each fold start using only earlier data, and the six test periods are joined into one 3.3-year out-of-sample record. Signals use the bar close and fill at the next open. Single-ticker strategies start each ticker with $10,000 ($100,000 for the _100k variants) and report the equal-weight portfolio; portfolio strategies run one $100,000 book, and shorts pay 50 bp a year. The luck bar is the Sharpe that the best of 389 zero-edge configurations would reach by chance over this length (Bailey & López de Prado 2014). Results are pre-tax and not investment advice. Several strategies trade US ETFs, which EU retail accounts cannot buy (PRIIPs).
