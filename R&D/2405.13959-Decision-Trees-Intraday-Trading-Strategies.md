# Decision Trees for Intuitive Intraday Trading Strategies

**Authors:** Prajwal Naga, Dinesh Balivada, Sharath Chandra Nirmala, Poornoday Tiruveedi  
**Affiliation:** Department of CSE, The National Institute of Engineering, Mysuru, India (undergraduate students)  
**arXiv:** [2405.13959](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.13959)  
**Date:** 22 May 2024  
**Full text:** [papers/2405.13959-Decision-Trees-Intraday-Trading-Strategies.md](papers/2405.13959-Decision-Trees-Intraday-Trading-Strategies.md)

## Abstract (paraphrased)
The paper trains a decision tree per stock on technical indicators to generate intraday trading
rules for individual NIFTY50 equities, instead of using a fixed hand-written rule set. The authors
report that the strategies beat buy-and-hold for many, but not all, stocks, and suggest backtesting
each stock before using its rules.

## Method
- **Data:** 1-minute bars, 2022 to 2024, from ICICI Breeze, adjusted with Yahoo Finance daily
  adjustment factors. Training 2022 to 2023, testing 2023 to 2024.
- **Features (9):** close return, 15-period return, RSI(14), ADX(14), SMA(14)/close ratio,
  correlation between SMA(14) and close, 14-period rolling volatility, 210-period rolling
  volatility (14-period volatility of 15-period returns), and rolling VWAP(14)/close ratio.
  (No MACD or Bollinger Bands.)
- **Target:** sign of the next bar's close return (1 = up, 0 = down).
- **Model:** scikit-learn `DecisionTreeClassifier`, Gini, max depth 4. Most trees use 5 to 7 features.
- **Backtest:** vectorbt, signals shifted one bar, returns on close prices.

## Results (Table 1, test period)
| | Buy and hold | Strategy |
|---|---|---|
| Total return | 34.2% | 28.6% |
| Sharpe | 2.36 | 5.81 |
| Max drawdown | -7.0% | -2.6% |
| Profit factor | 1.63 | 1.11 |
| Win rate | | 48.2% |

Stocks beating buy-and-hold on test data: 46% on total return, 60% on Sharpe (80% and 84% in training).

## Caveats
- **No commissions or slippage** ("not explicitly incorporated"). With 1-minute signals, a 48% win
  rate and a profit factor of 1.11, realistic costs would very likely erase the result.
- On the test period the strategy returned less than buy-and-hold; the higher Sharpe mostly reflects
  lower exposure and volatility.
- Missing bars are filled by linear interpolation, which uses later prices (minor look-ahead).
- The conclusion proposes dropping stocks that underperformed, which is selection on the test set.
- Short student paper, single train/test split, no walk-forward.

## Relevance to algotrader
The most directly testable of these papers: single-stock minute bars, and features computable from
our data. Worth implementing as a `Strategy` and testing with our cost model before drawing conclusions.
