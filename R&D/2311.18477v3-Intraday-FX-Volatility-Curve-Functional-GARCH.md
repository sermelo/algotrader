# Intraday FX Volatility-Curve Forecasting with Functional GARCH Approaches

**Authors:** Fearghal Kearney, Han Lin Shang, Yuqian Zhao  
**Affiliation:** Queen's University Belfast (UK), Macquarie University (Australia), University of Sussex (UK)  
**arXiv:** [2311.18477v3](https://arxiv.org/abs/2311.18477v3)  
**Date:** v1 30 Nov 2023, v3 30 Sep 2025  
**Full text:** [papers/2311.18477v3-Intraday-FX-Volatility-Curve-Functional-GARCH.md](papers/2311.18477v3-Intraday-FX-Volatility-Curve-Functional-GARCH.md)

## Abstract (paraphrased)
Forecasts intraday volatility curves for USD/EUR, USD/GBP and USD/JPY with functional GARCH models.
Intraday return curves of the three currencies are strongly cross-dependent and show long-range
conditional heteroskedasticity. Modelling that dependence with multi-level functional principal
component analysis, and adding intraday bid-ask spread curves as exogenous inputs, improves intraday
and day-ahead forecasts over functional and realised-volatility baselines. The forecasts are turned
into intraday Value-at-Risk (VaR) curves, which the authors show can limit large losses in an
intraday trading strategy.

## Summary
Each day's intraday return path is treated as one functional observation and modelled with
GARCH-type dynamics, after dimension reduction (long-range-dependent or multi-level FPCA). Section 5
tests a simple strategy: open at the predicted intraday minimum (or maximum, for shorts) and close
at the other extreme, skipping days with small predicted ranges and exiting when the realised path
hits the VaR curve. Transaction cost is set at 0.0003%.

## Caveats
- A risk/volatility forecasting paper; the trading strategy is an illustration, not the contribution.
- FX, not equities; the cost assumption is very low.

## Relevance to algotrader
Indirect. Intraday volatility or VaR forecasts could replace our simple ATR stop and position sizing,
but the method is heavy to implement and gives no signal on its own.
